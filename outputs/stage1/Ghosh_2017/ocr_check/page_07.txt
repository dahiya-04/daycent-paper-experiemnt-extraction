In long-run, alternate-year inclusion of chickpea in
cereal—cereal rotation increased the TOC content that
might be associated with legume effect such as higher
belowground biomass, leaf fall, and higher rhizodeposition
of C. In parallel, Ganeshamurthy [5] reported that pulse
inclusive rotations maintained higher SOC due to addition
of more below ground biomass in the form of roots. Fur-
ther, a considerable portion of legume roots (~ 43-47%)
are non-decomposable that finally contributes to SOC
build-up [19]. On the other hand, in the present study,
cereals were highly responsive to inorganic fertilizers, and
thus its growth was severely reduced in control and organic
amended plots. In contrast, chickpea being a leguminous
N-fixing crop did not response much too nutrient man-
agement. Therefore, the variation in crop biomass with
nutrient management was marginal in chickpea. Conse-
quently, higher crop residues were returned in organic
nutrient management in chickpea included rotations
(Table 5), which directly influenced to the total C-input.
The higher content of all C-fractions under organic treat-
ment is due higher C-input in the form of FYM and crop
residue. In parallel, earlier reports from this region speci-
fied that combined use of organic manure with or without
inorganic fertilizer are superior in enriching TOC and its
fractions than chemical fertilizer application alone [20].
The study also indicates the potential of inorganic fertil-
ization in SOC sequestration. Higher enrichment of SOC
and C-fractions in surface soil (O-0.2 m) was due to
addition of C to the surface soil and a large part of plant
root is also confined to the surface soil. The different

fractions of TOC (Cfrac\-Cfrac4) are very dynamic
parameter in soil system. Subsequently, in the investiga-
tion, the higher variations were evident within the repli-
cations among different treatments. Crop rotation effect on
C-fractions was just restricted to very-labile Cfrac, and
labile Cfracz; this might be due to higher addition/input of
hydrolysable and readily mineralisable C that enhances the
microbial activity in surface soil layers [21]. Lefroy et al.
[22] observed similar variations in Cfrac,, Cfracz and
Cfrac3 which indicated that these fractions were basically
responding to crop and soil management. That may be the
reason; these three fractions are fundamentally utilized for
derivation of LI as delineated by Blair et al. [17]. Organic
amendments like crop residue and FYM are easily miner-
alized under a subtropical condition. Therefore, their effect
was visible only on labile C-fractions (Cfrac,, Cfracz and
Cfrac3).

Active and Passive C-Pools

The inclusion of chickpea in RW and MW system improved
the active C-pool in the surface soil. However, crop rotation
had no significant (P > 0.05) effect on passive C-pool
(Fig. 2). In both the production system, the content of active
C-pool in surface soil was found in the order of
organic > inorganic > control. The proportion of active
C-pool in surface (O-0.2 m) to sub-surface (0.2-0.4 m) soil
was significantly (P < 0.05) increased with the inclusion of
chickpea. The mean ratio (surface: sub-surface) was higher
