Abstract Continuous practices of cereal-cereal rotations
in Indo-Gangetic plain (IGP) region have emerged with
several soil related issues including depletion of soil
organic carbon (SOC). Crop diversification remains a
conceivable option to address this issue. Here, the authors
envisaged the long-term alternate-year inclusion of chick-
pea in upland maize—wheat and lowland rice—wheat sys-
tems on SOC, C-dynamics and C management indices
under organic, inorganic, and control nutrient management.
Inclusion of chickpea in rice-wheat and maize—wheat
systems increased total organic C (TOC) by 6.5 and 13.4%,
and particulate organic C (POC) by 94.7 and 41.3% in the
surface soil (0O-0.2 m), respectively. In both the production
systems, enrichment of very-labile C (Cfrac,) and labile C
(Cfracz) fractions were apparent with inclusion of chick-
pea. Very-labile (Cfrac,) (36.6—37.9%) in upland and less-
labile (Cfrac3) (29.1-31.7%) in lowland were the dominant
C-fractions. Subsequently, upland conditions had higher

Introduction

Indo-Gangetic Plains (IGP) is designated as ‘bread basket’
of South Asia and continuous cereal—cereal rotations are
typical in this zone. Rice (Oryza sativa L.)—wheat (Triti-
cum aestivum L.) (RW) and maize (Zea mays L.)—wheat
(MW) are two most important rotations of IGP contributing
largely to national food grain production [1]. However,
cereal—cereal rotations of IGP are presently confronting
several second generation issues related to soil, environ-
ment, and natural resource degradation that in turn affect
the production sustainability [2]. Depletion of soil organic
carbon (SOC) and continuous exhaustion of soil nutrients
have been identified as major stumbling blocks to crop
productivity in this region [3]. Numerous analysts under-
scored that lack of crop diversity further aggravated the
problems [4]. Therefore, in order to ensure food security of
