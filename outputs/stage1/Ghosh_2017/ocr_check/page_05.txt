Results and Discussion
Carbon Fractions and Particulate Organic C

Chickpea inclusion in cereal—cereal rotations (RW and
MW) significantly (P < 0.05) increased the TOC. In sur-
face soil (0O-0.2 m), inclusion of chickpea in RW and MW
rotation increased the TOC by 6.5 and 13.5%, respectively
(Tables 3, 4). The inclusion of chickpea in lowland RW
and upland MW system increased the very-labile C-frac-
tion (Cfrac,) at surface soil depth (O-0.2 m), but in lower
soil depths it was marginal. The content of Cfrac,; was
found higher in upland when compared with lowland.
Chickpea inclusion in alternate-year increased labile
C-fraction (Cfrac2) in both the production systems and the
effect was likewise limited to surface soil. With inclusion
of chickpea, the enrichment (P < 0.05) of Cfrac3 was
observed only in the upland system. In the study, non-labile
C-fraction of TOC (Cfrac,4) did not differ (P > 0.05) with
crop rotation. In upland, the share of different C-fractions
followed the order Cfrac, > Cfracy > Cfracy > Cfrac3
and Cfrac,; > Cfracz > Cfracy > Cfracy in the surface

In both the production systems, addition of FYM at
5 Mg ha! + incorporation of crop residue + bio-fertil-
izers (organic nutrient management) resulted in higher
content of all C-fractions (Cfrac,, Cfrac2, Cfrac3, and
Cfrac4) followed by balanced inorganic fertilization
(Tables 3, 4). Subsequently, higher values of TOC in sur-
face soil were registered in organic than inorganic and
control plots. Irrespective of the production system, nutri-
ent management effect on the quantitative enrichment of
C-fractions was in the order of Cfrac,; > Cfracz > -
Cfrac3 > Cfrac4. In the lower soil depths, nutrient man-
agement effect was marginal (P > 0.05); however,
enrichment of Cfrac, was noticed even in sub-surface soil
in organic treatment plots. The POC content in RWRC and
MWMC was higher (P < 0.05) over traditional RW and
MW systems, indicating POC is also a sensitive C-fraction
to soil and crop management. The average POC value was
almost 2 times higher in upland over lowland soil. Among
the nutrient management practices, the POC content was in
the order of organic > inorganic (NPKSZnB) > control

(Fig. 1).
