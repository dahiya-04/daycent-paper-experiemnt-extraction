Soil Sampling and Analysis

Soil samples were collected from four soil depths (O-0.2,
0.2-0.4, 0.4-0.6, 0.6—-0.8 m). Soil samples were taken with
post-hole auger from four random spots in each plot (three
replications) after harvesting the last crop in the crop cycle.
The sampled soils were air dried, ground followed by
passing through a 2 mm sieve and stored at room tem-
perature for analyzing oxidisable organic C by wet oxida-
tion method [12]. Total organic C was calculated using the
formula: oxidizable organic carbon x 1.33 [13]. In surface
and sub-surface depths, organic C-fractions were analyzed
through modified Walkley and Black method as described
by Chan et al. [14]. The concentrated H2SO, of 5, 10 and
20 ml resulted in three acid aqueous solution ratios of
0.5:1, 1:1, and 2:1 corresponding to 12 N, 18 N and 24 N

of H,SO,, respectively [15]. The organic C determined
using 5, 10 and 20 ml of concentrated H2SO,4, when
compared with TOC allowed separation of TOC into fol-
lowing four different fractions of decreasing oxidisability:
fraction I (Cfrac,), organic C oxidisable under 12 N
H,SO,; fraction Il (Cfrac2), the difference in organic C
extracted between 18 N and 12 N H,SO,; fraction II
(Cfrac3), the difference in organic C extracted between
18 N and 24 N H,SO, (24 N H2SOy, is equivalent to the
standard Walkley and Black method); and fraction IV
(Cfrac4), residual organic C after reaction with 24 N
H,SO, when compared with TOC [16].

Carbon Management Index (CMI)

Carbon management index was calculated using the fol-
lowing formulas.

First, lability index (LI) was determined using three of
the C-fractions (Cfrac,, Cfracy and Cfrac3) as mentioned
earlier. Subsequently, a weightage of 3, 2 and 1 was given
to Cfrac,, Cfracz, and Cfrac3, respectively and their actual
values were transformed to a proportional amount of TOC
and weighed with the weighing factor to get a lability index
for the organic C content [17].
