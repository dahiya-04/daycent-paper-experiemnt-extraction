highly complex due to urea amendments in conjunction with dif-
ferent quantities and times of crop residue incorporation. Hence,
no observable relationship between the assessed parameters could
come out that may describe N20 fluxes in a decisive manner.

3.5. Yield

Continuous no tilling led to reduction in yield during both
the years. However, the response of yield was different dur-
ing the two years trial (Fig. 8). In 2009, grain weight followed
the trend RCT-WCT > RNT-WCT > RNT-WNT > RCT-WNT. But during
2010 the order was: RCT-WCT ~ RCT-WNT > RNT-WCT ~ RNT-
WNT. It reflects that in complete absence of any disturbance
(RNT-WNT), changes in physical properties of soil did not allow

plants to get benefited as reflected by indicators of plant growth
(root weight, grain weight and tiller numbers). But slight distur-
bance and its order played role. Added residues of the previous
wheat crop under no tillage but less compaction under RNT-
WCT made nutritional provisions easily available to plants through
improved aeration, tilth, root penetration, etc. Hence yield was
comparable or slightly less in RNT-WCT than that of conventional
practice during the two years of the experiment. Yield reduc-
tions were high in RCT-WNT and RNT-WNT leading to make these
treatments less productive during both the years. This could be
attributed to the higher weed infestation in spite of the herbicide
application of pendimethalin (1 kg ha~!) followed by one manual
weeding at 30DAS. The results clearly reflect variations in soil
qualities subject to degree and time of tillage for such behaviour
of different tillage practices. Since most of the studies reported
so far evaluate only tillage or no tillage, comparison of our alter-
nately tilled fields could not be made with any other similar
system. Lower yield under no tillage has also been reported pre-
viously in rice-wheat system (Hobbs et al., 2008). However, Dong
et al. (2010) found similar or improved grain production under no
tillage in rice-rice cultivation practice. Regarding stability of yields,
RNT-WNT was resistant to annual variations as reflected by no sig-
nificant differences in the yield observed between 2009 and 2010.
This observation complied well with Qin et al. (2010). Effect of
tillage practices on yield discards our hypothesis that yield will
remain unaffected under any tillage practice if all recommended
fertilizer doses, and water requirements are provided with due crop
care. Yield in all the tillage practices were higher in 2010 than in
2009 except for RNT-WNT in which no significant difference in yield
was observed. Favourable meteorological conditions during 2010
appear to be the reason for this. But for RNT-WNT, environmen-
tal conditions did no significant benefit to the crop yield. Effect
of changes in soil conditions apparently dominated over the ben-
efit of improved ambient conditions during 2010, reflecting that
long term behaviour of no tillage system needs to be studied more
extensively.

3.6. Identification of probable optimum performer

When all the GHG emissions were converted into COz-e over 100
year time horizon, it was evident that emissions of N20 were the
lowest while that of CH, was the highest. The results clearly indi-
cated that fluxes of CH, and N20 were reduced but CO increased
maximally in RNT-WNT. In lieu of high global warming poten-
tials of CH4 and N20, and keeping in mind the observed tradeoff
between CH, and CO;, reduction of CH, and N2O should be under
focus. The most environment friendly practice was therefore RNT-
WNT and the worst was RCT-WCT. But adoption of RNT-WNT is not
recommendable for its significant negative impact on yield. How-
ever, with more effective and efficient weed management using
pre and post emergence herbicide application the yield reduc-
tions can be minimized. In light of the observations of the present
study, RNT-WCT can be regarded as the best practice among the
four as yield gaps between RNT-WCT and RCT-WCT were the low-
est (in 2009) or not significant (in 2010) and can be narrowed
further if causes of yield reductions under this practice could be
identified.

Nevertheless, more studies are required with more tillage
permutations and other amendments to verify before any recom-
mendation can be made at large.

4. Conclusion

Reduction in tillage frequency led to suppression of CH4 and N20
but increased CO fluxes.
