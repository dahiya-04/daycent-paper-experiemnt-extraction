3. Results and discussion
3.1. Soil properties

During six years of continuous treatments of tillage permuta-
tions in rice-wheat system, significant variations in bulk density,
organic C, N and P contents in soil of alternately and no tilled
fields have occurred (Table 1). Redox potential of soil under all
the tillage treatments decreased gradually since transplantation,
becoming negative under flooded conditions and reaching min-
imum by 20-25 DAT (Fig. 3). The minimum ORP was recorded
in RCT-WCT (—165 410.8). As water levels in the fields declined
and soils tended towards drier conditions, ORP also approached
towards positive values indicating that the soil conditions gradu-
ally became oxidizing. Water occupies voids present between soil
particles thus removing and preventing accumulation of air (and
hence O2) in addition of cutting atmospheric oxygen supply. This
causes reduction potential to fall when water logging continues so
that most of the oxidizing agents present in soils get reduced. Soil
organic carbon, total N and available P increased with reduction in
tillage intensity. This is expected due to crop residue incorporation
with less disturbance of soil, hence decreased oxidation of organic
matter. Similar observations were reported by Mina et al. (2008)
and Wang et al. (2008). In 2010, however, P was observed to be
lower in RNT-WNT than rest of the treatments (data not presented
for 2010). Since soil sampling was done on the day of land prepa-
ration, it may be possible that in RNT-WNT, P did not mix properly
with soil due to residue of previous crop.

3.2. Temporal variations in fluxes of CH4, N20 and CO2 during
different stages of plant development

3.2.1. Methane

In all the treatments, CH, fluxes followed similar temporal
pattern, with two peaks (Fig. 3). In 2009, emissions rose since
transplantation reaching first maximum near 40DAT and then
decreasing gradually till 50DAT. After this, fluxes again started
increasing and near 70DAT second maximum was reached. The
emissions subsided by 80 DAT, gradually decreasing to zero some-
times becoming undetectable. The second peak was much broader
in RCT-WCT as the emissions remained high (~600 mg m~? h~') for
longer period (60-80 DAT) as compared to rest of the plots where
peak was sharp (~70 DAT), but higher (~1700 mg m? h7! in RCT-
WNT and RNT-WCT and ~1200 mg m~2 h7! in RNT-WNT). In 2010,
the temporal pattern was similar, but the peaks shifted towards left
and fluxes were lower. The first maximum of CH, flux appeared at
13-18 DAT except in RCT-WNT in which peak appeared at 23 DAT.
The second maximum was recorded near 60 DAT in RCT-WNT and
RNT-WCT; 70 DAT in RNT-WNT while in RCT-WCT, peak was flat-
tened (40-60 DAT) similar to that observed during 2009. In all the
cases, first peak was higher than the second.

Highest CH, fluxes were observed during the tillering stage
which is in agreement with Gogoi et al. (2005), Ahmad et al. (2009),
and Li et al. (2011). Due to plant growth and development of new
tillers, root exudation rate is high during this phase, which increases
CH, generation by supplying substrates for methanogenesis (Singh
et al., 2009). Previous studies concluded that about half of the
CH, production in rice plants is driven by roots through exuda-
tion or their decay (Watanabe et al., 1999). The generated CHy, is
transported towards atmosphere by the rice tillers through their
aerenchyma, thus reducing chances of oxidation by methanotropic
activity in near surface soil (aerobic zone) (Sass and Cicerone,

completely developed thereby causing reduction in root exudation.
Plant loses some of the tillers whereas conduit effects of existing
tillers diminish due to ageing (Singh et al., 2009). This decreases
CH, production as well as its transport via the plant. During the
diffusion of CH, through the soil methanotropic bacteria which are
ubiquitous in agricultural fields and may oxidize up to 90% of the
generated CH, (Mer and Roger, 2001). These bacteria are active
in aerobic rhizosphere and soil water/soil atmosphere interface.
Additionally, after tillering, flooding was removed and moderate
moisture was maintained either by rainfall or supplementary irri-
gation. Therefore anaerobic conditions weakened causing gradual
reduction in CH, fluxes. Fluxes of CH, from all the tillage practices
were higher during 2009 than in 2010 as is reflected in cumulative
emissions in different stages of plant for two years (Fig. 5). Three-
way ANOVA results (Table 2) also indicate that CH4 emissions in
different life stages of rice plants were influenced by year of mea-
surement, phase of plant and interaction of the two. Cumulative
CH, emissions in all the tillage practices were different in 2009
and 2010 during reproductive and maturity stages but were sim-
ilar during vegetative phase except for RNT-WCT. This indicates
that the factors responsible for creating the difference in fluxes
did not influence much the vegetative phase of the crop. Up to
tillering stage when flooding is maintained, meteorological condi-
tions (particularly rainfall) have little effect on plant growth (Corton
et al., 2000) and no influence on soil processes. But when flooding
is terminated and moisture is maintained through rain or sup-
plementary irrigation, rainfall influences rice plant as well as soil
conditions significantly. As is clear from Fig. 2 rainfall was more and
better distributed during 2010 (771.9mm) as compared to 2009
(462.0 mm). Increased rainfall during 2010 did not cause flooding
for more than a day but created wetting and drying cycles dur-
ing reproductive and maturity stages of rice which reportedly can
suppress methanogenesis with probable improvement in methan-
otropy by carrying dissolved oxygen into the soil (Pathak et al.,
2003). Such environmental conditions are favourable for spikelet
formation and grain filling and therefore mediate translocation of
photosynthates to spikelets thus reducing allocation to roots. This
cuts off carbon substrate supply to the soil through root exudation
thus leading to decreased CH, generation, but increase the grain
weight (Sass and Cicerone, 2002).

3.2.2. Nitrous oxide

Fluxes of N20 generally remained positive over the entire crop
season and were highly variable (Fig. 4). Fluxes were lower during
the flooded conditions, and increased as soil flooding was stopped
but moderate moisture was maintained. Under prevailing anaer-
obic conditions during flooding, denitrification dominates over
nitrification, which enhances the probability of NO reduction to
N2 in case the process goes to completion (Wrage et al., 2001).
