This could have led to the observed differences during maturity and
reproductive stages in the two years.

3.2.3. Carbon dioxide

Although there was no particular temporal trend, CO2 fluxes
in general were higher in later stages of crop development (after
70 DAT). On most of the monitoring days, CO2 fluxes were posi-
tive (Fig. 4) and high with maximum flux recorded in RNT-WNT
during 2010 (502 +31.2 mgm~7h~') and RCT-WNT during 2009
(400 + 20.16 mg m~? h~). Initially for 3-5 days after transplanta-
tion, CO2 fluxes remained high but afterwards decreased suddenly
to near zero and sometimes negative. During 2009, fluxes were pos-
itive in RCT-WNT and RNT-WNT but negative fluxes continued in
RCT-WCT and RNT-WCT up to 70-75 DAT. Observations are similar
to those of Mitaya et al. (2000) and Li et al. (2010). During 2010,
fluxes remained positive throughout except on two monitoring
dates. Negative fluxes indicate that soil absorbed atmospheric CO3.
This could be attributable to anaerobicity in soil due to floodwater
which led to sharp decline in aerobic respiration (Li et al., 2010).
