The overall findings suggest that changes in soil physical prop-

3.4, Relationship between fluxes of greenhouse gases and soil
parameters

CH, fluxes had an inverse relationship with ORP, while a
direct relationship was obtained with moisture content in all the
tillage practices (Fig. 7). A linear dependency in both the cases
was observed. The regression equations defining the dependencies
were different for the four practices. These variations may have
originated due to differences in soil qualities; plant attributes as
well as changes in microbial phenomena as modulated by differ-
ent tillage permutations. The value of slope of regression equation

derived between CH, flux and reduction potential indicated the
change in CH, flux per unit change in reduction potential and was
found to be the maximum in RCT-WNT while the minimum was in
RNT-WCT. Although no clear statistical correlation between mois-
ture content and reduction potential could be obtained, we expect
a co-dependency of ORP and CH, fluxes on moisture content. As
water expels out the air trapped in the soil pores, under saturation
complete absence of molecular oxygen takes place. It is reported
widely that methanogenesis occurs below —150 mV, but we noticed
CH, emissions at much higher values of ORP. It is possible that
soil at greater depths became more anaerobic while our ORP mea-
surement was limited up to 10cm depth only where soil might
be relatively oxidizing. This is evident from numerous studies car-
ried out in rice fields that soil water interface up to rhizosphere
remain aerobic for most of the time due to plant mediated diffu-
sion of O2 into this zone. Also, regeneration of oxidants in this zone
is rapid and hence removal of flood water can immediately regen-
