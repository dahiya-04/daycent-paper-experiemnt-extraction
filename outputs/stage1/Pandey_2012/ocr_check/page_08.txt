3.3. Effects of tillage permutations on fluxes of CH4, N20 and CO

Results of three-way ANOVA (Table 2) indicate that tillage prac-
tices affected cumulative emission rates of all the GHGs in different
stages of rice plants. Most of the times, fluxes of CH, and N30
remained low in alternately tilled and no tilled fields although tem-
poral trends were similar in all the treatments. This was against
our hypothesis 1. Since fluxes are the net outcome of generation
and consumption of the gas, it is the relative strengths of the two
processes which lead to the observed trends. Reduction in tillage
(RNT-WNT followed by RCT-WNT and RNT-WCT) protects organic
matter from microbial attack in soil aggregates which could have
reduced the productions of CH, and N20 (Hanaki et al., 2002; Ali
et al., 2009; Ahmad et al., 2009). Another important factor is gas dif-
fusivity which depends inversely on bulk density. Therefore gases
which are generated at greater soil depths take more time to travel
towards the atmosphere in RNT-WNT, RNT-WCT and RCT-WNT
than in RCT-WCT. This provides more time to consumers to act on
the gaseous molecules thereby improving efficiency of consump-
tion (Ahmadetal., 2009; Alietal., 2009). In all the tillage treatments,
methanogenesis could be comparable as reflected by similar ORP
values. Consumption, however, in alternately and never tilled soils
(RNT-WCT, RCT-WNT and RNT-WNT) might take place at such a
rate that it compensated generation relatively to larger extent thus
ultimately leading to fluxes lower than RCT-WCT. Previous studies
have shown strong positive correlations between methanogenesis
and methanotropy for the reason that CH, availability may fos-
ter methanotropic population/activity provided no other resource
becomes limiting (Mer and Roger, 2001). Generally, N becomes
limiting to methanotropy (Zhang et al., 2012). With reduction in
tillage frequency, N content in soils increased significantly (Table 1).
Hence, methanotropy could be better supported in RNT-WNT, RNT-
WCT and RCT-WNT as compared to RCT-WCT.

The chances of CH, oxidation decline when major fraction of
CH, bypasses through rice plants. Total number of tillers formed per
unit area was significantly greater in RCT-WCT and RNT-WCT than
in RCT-WNT and RNT-WNT (Fig. 6). Hence, apparently in RCT-WNT
and RNT-WNT, the probability of CH, emission routed through
plant decreased as compared to RCT-WCT and RCT-WNT. Positive
correlation between tiller numbers and CH, fluxes have widely
been observed and discussed (Gogoi et al., 2005). Reduced root
weight in RNT-WNT and RCT-WNT (Fig. 6) also pointed towards
decline in plant mediated gaseous transport owing to reduced sur-
face area and depth of root penetration into the soil. Reduced tillers
as well as root weight indicate poor plant growths in RCT-WNT and
RNT-WNT.

Like CH4, N20 consumption during its movement through soil is
expected to become efficient under compact soils (RNT-WNT, RNT-
WCT and RCT-WNT), due to delayed movement towards the soil-air
interphase. The consumptive processes of N20 are diverse but den-
itrification is the major one. Denitrification near surface in soils
with better aggregation (under alternately and never tilled soils)
may improve due to creation of anaerobic microsites (Baggs et al.,
2003). Besides this, nitrifying bacteria (in aerobic soils) may also
consume a part of N20 produced through a process called nitrifier
denitrification (Beaumont et al., 2004), while several bacteria per-
form aerobic denitrification under aerobic conditions also. Hence,
it is very difficult to speculate in the present study that which N20
consuming group is playing the major role, but reduced velocity of
N2O escape is expected to improve efficiency of consumption. In
addition chances of dissolution of N2O in soil solution and leaching
may also be appreciable in non puddled RNT-WCT and RNT-WNT
(Clough et al., 2005; Chapuis-Lardy et al., 2007). This may reduce
emission of N2O into the atmosphere. Exact partitioning of sources
and sinks of N2O that contributed to the observed fluxes was not
made in the present study, but overall effect of reduced tillage was

Contrary to the trends observed for N20 and CHy, CO2 fluxes
were highest in RCT-WNT followed by RNT-WNT, RNT-WCT and
RCT-WCT in 2009. Chief source of CO2 is aerobic soil respiration. As
discussed previously that near surface, soil and water remains aer-
obic and microbial processes in these zones indispensably release
CO2. If methanotrophy rate was higher in aerobic zones of RNT-
WNT, RNT-WCT and RCT-WNT than in RCT-WCT as expected, CH4
oxidation adds to general soil respiration, possibly increasing the
magnitude of CO2 fluxes. However, tillage leads to better mixing of
residues and promotes soil respiration (Lal, 2004). The net outcome
of the above two effects of tillage led to the observed fluxes. Li et al.
(2010) reported that no tillage increases the emissions of CO3 in rice
fields, while many studies reported that no tillage reduced or had no
effect on CO2 fluxes (Ussiri and Lal, 2009). The studies available in
scientific literature only compared tillage and no tillage practices in
various systems. However, our study takes into account all possible
tillage arrangements in rice-wheat cultivation system. CO2 fluxes
