Greenhouse gas emissions from rice crop with different tillage permutations
in rice-wheat system

Divya Pandey, Madhoolika Agrawal, Jitendra Singh Bohra

* Laboratory of Air Pollution & Global Climate Change, Department of Botany, Banaras Hindu University, Varanasi, India
b Department of Agronomy, Institute of Agricultural Sciences, Banaras Hindu University, Varanasi, India

ARTICLE INFO ABSTRACT

Article history:

Received 3 January 2012

Received in revised form 9 June 2012
Accepted 7 July 2012

Global agriculture lies in intersection of two inescapable issues of the present times, viz. keeping pace
with growing food demand and participating in climate change mitigation efforts. Rice cultivation is a
major emitter of greenhouse gases (GHGs) and no tillage of soil is becoming popular due to low economic
investments with improved soil conditions. However, how these arrangements will affect GHG emissions
need to be quantified. Therefore, monitoring over diverse agricultural practices is essential for optimum
utilization of cultivable land and resources, while identifying the chances of emission reductions. We
assessed the impacts of four tillage practices in rice-wheat cultivation system on fluxes of GHGs (CHa,
N2O and CO2) and yield of rice. The tillage practices were tilling of soil before sowing of every crop
(RCT-WCT), tillage before sowing of rice but no tillage before sowing of wheat (RCT-WNT), tillage before
sowing of wheat but no tillage before sowing of rice (RNT-WCT), and no tillage before sowing of rice
as well as wheat (RNT-WNT). Reduction in tillage frequency led to significant reductions in fluxes of
CH, and N20, but increased CO2 while permutations of tillage and no tillage influenced grain yield.
RNT-WCT produced next to or comparable to the most yielding RCT-WCT. Although it is difficult to
select the best performer since no single tillage permutation showed consistent increment in yield with
accompanied emission reductions, RNT-WCT, however may be considered as better agricultural practice
for the study region. Long term and extensive spatio-temporal monitoring is still required before making
any recommendation. It is also essential for understanding the factors that cause declination in yields
under no tillage.

1. Introduction

Being the largest anthropogenic impact on the earth in terms
of the land area occupied, agriculture also contributes maximally
to the greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions (Foley et al., 2011; Gilbert,
2011). As global population grows with projected doubling of food
demand by 2050, associated GHG contributions from this sector
will also rise (Gilbert, 2011). Minimizing the negative environmen-
tal impacts of agriculture hence is a challenge. To address the issue,
assessment of different management options with respect to their
responses of GHG emission and yield is required. Rice cultivation,
particularly under puddled condition has received due attention
owing to their high CH, contribution and large geographical cover-
age (Beerling et al., 2007; Maraseniet al., 2009; Eusufzai et al., 2010;

Power, 2010; Zhang et al., 2011). Conventional cultivation practices
and modifications made therein have been evaluated extensively in
this regard. Popular strategies for yield enhancements like nutrient
application (inorganic and organic) often increase GHG emissions
which are again highly dependent on the nature, quantity and
timing of application (Pathak et al., 2002). Organic manure like
farmyard manure and green manure increased CHy, N20 and CO2
emissions in rice field by providing extra substrate to responsible
microbes (Bhatia et al., 2005).

Attempts have been made to suppress GHG production in soils
through chemical inhibiters like calcium carbide, dicyandiamide,
nitapyrin and thiosulphate (Kumar et al., 2000; Pathak et al., 2002),
their adoption is less feasible due to cost intensiveness and possi-
ble unforeseen environmental impacts. Irrigation management is
effective in mitigating CH, without affecting yield but concomi-
tant rise in N20 emissions warrant more research (Li et al., 2005).
