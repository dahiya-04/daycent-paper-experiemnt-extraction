D. Pandey et al. / Agriculture, Ecosystems and Environment 159 (2012) 133-144 143

Our hypothesis, therefore, that in RNT-WNT, highest CH, emis-
sions will take place was not supported but as hypothesized that
alternate tillage will induce high COz was supported in case of RCT-
WNT. An important result was that yield could not be maintained by
just providing recommended nutrients and due care to the crop but
changes in soil characteristics during a considerable term of tillage
practices left a significant influence. The exact reasons behind the
limiting factors under such management practices thus need atten-
tion for improving yield under desired management practices. It is
too early to suggest any single optimum performer in light of the
observations of only two year study, but RNT-WCT may offer oppor-
tunities to be explored further as a better cultivation practice, in lieu
of significant emission reductions and chances of bringing its yield
comparable to the conventional practice.
