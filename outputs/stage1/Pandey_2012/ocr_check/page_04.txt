2.4, Tiller number root weight and grain weight

25cm x 25 cm areas were marked at six places randomly in each
plot. Total number of tillers in each marked area was counted at 30

days after transplantation (DAT) in RCT-WCT and RCT-WNT cor-
responding to 56-59 days after sowing (DAS) in RNT-WCT and
RNT-WNT. At this stage, rice plants achieve maximum number of
tillers. After this, some of the tillers die. Harvests from each of the
experimental plot were bundled separately and tagged. Bundles
were dried in open under the sun for one week. After the grains
dried, threshing was done and grain yield plot-! was recorded and
converted to yield ha~!. Root weight was determined on the date of
harvest. For this, plants in 25 cm x 25 cmarea were taken out at four
places at random with soil cores. Roots were washed thoroughly,
dried and weighed.

2.5. Soil sampling and analysis

Oxido-reduction potential (ORP) of soil was measured in 2010
using Double junction ORP Tester 10 (Eutech Instruments, Mum-
bai, India). The instrument was first calibrated in the laboratory
with reference Zobbel buffer to 245 mV. Soil temperature was mea-
sured with the help of a mercury thermometer (1/10°C). Both
measurements were taken at 10cm depth. For bulk density soil was
collected up to 15cm depth using the soil corer (6.5 cm diameter).
For other analyses, soils from 15 cm depth were collected from four
different places randomly in each treatment with the help of auger.
The collected soils were mixed together, a subsample was used for
the analyses of moisture content and rest was air dried and ground
to pass through 2 mm sieve. Soil organic carbon and total nitrogen
were determined following Walkley and Black (1947) and micro
Kjeldahl digestion method of Bremner and Mulvaney (1982) using
Gerhardt Automatic N Analyzer, Frankfurt, Germany, respectively.
Available phosphorous was extracted following Olsen et al. (1954)
and estimated by the method of Dickman and Bray (1940).
