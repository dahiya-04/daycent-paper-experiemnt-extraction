Soil strength (SS) or soil compaction was measured after harvest of each
rice and wheat crop to know the effect of tillage and crop-residue applica-
tion on crop-root development and crop growth (Hasegawa et al. 1985;
Busscher et al. 2000). The SS was measured at three randomly selected loca-
tions in each treatment plot and averaged to get more accurate values. Three
readings of SS at 2.54 cm, 7.62 cm, and 12.7 cm depth were measured from
each location. Soil strength measurement was performed with a manually
operated cone penetrometer equipped with a 30° cone having a 95-sq.-cm
basal area (Singh & Malhi 2006). All of the measurements of soil strength
were taken just after harvest of rice and wheat crops when moisture content
of all the soil layers was near field capacity.

Double ring infiltrometer was used to measure the infiltration rate of
the soil (Bouwer 1986). The diameters of outer and inner rings of the infil-
trometer were 30 and 55 cm, respectively. The infiltrometers were placed in
plots of all tillage-crop residue combinations after harvest of rice crop (2003)
and measurement was taken at 30, 60, 120, 150, 195, 210, 240, and 300 min,
until the steady-state infiltration rate was achieved.

Statistical Analyses

The effects of tillage, crop residue, and N application rate on the final status
of SOC were assessed by measuring soil organic carbon at the end of the
experimental period (October 2003) under all the treatment combinations
in both TR-W-F and DR-W-F. The data obtained were statistically analyzed
by M-STATC software package and presented in the form of analyses of
variance (ANOVA) (Russel & Einsensmith 1986). ANOVA was also performed
to assess the effect of tillage and crop-residue application on yield of rice and
wheat crops under average rate of inorganic N application. The differences
between treatment means were tested using critical difference value at 5%
(CDo.os) and 1% (CD01) levels of significance. Linear regression relationships
among soil organic carbon level and rate of N application were established
using Microsoft Excel (2003).
