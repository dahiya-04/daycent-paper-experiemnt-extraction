Application of rice crop residue in 2001-02 and application of rice and
wheat crop residues either singly or in combination in 2002-03 increased
the grain yield of wheat following TR and DR significantly. Incorporation
of rice and wheat crop residues increased the yield of the wheat crop
over the wheat without crop-residue treatment. Application of crop residues
increased the SOC level (Figures 2 and 3), which helped in retaining the soil
nutrients, and reduced soil strength (Figures 6 and 7), which helped root
growth of succeeding wheat growth in nearly dry and unsaturated soil envi-
ronment following wet, massive rice soil environment. The application of
crop residues also minimized the water loss by evaporation, improved water
infiltration by reducing run-off, and decreased nitrate-N leaching (Munawar
et al. 1990; Shipitalo, Dick, & Edwards 2000; Sarkar 2005).

The results of yields of both the TR-W-F and DR-W-F systems revealed
that intensive and less intensive either wet or dry tillage had prominent
effect on rice and wheat production. The intensity of both wet and dry
tillage (T; in TR, T; in DR and t,in wheat) was effective in reducing water
loss, thus increasing yield of crops, but it had malefic effect on the soil’s
physical properties and soil organic carbon content. Incorporation of crop
residues was proven as a suitable measure for augmenting organic carbon
and productivity of soil as well as in maintaining long-term stability and
ecological and economical sustainability of rice-wheat system (Sarkar & Kar
2006; Sarkar & Kar 2008).
