METHODS AND MATERIALS
Experimental Site

A field investigation was carried out at the experimental farm of
Agricultural & Food Engineering Department, Indian Institute of Technology,
Kharagpur, India. Kharagpur (22°19’N latitude; 87°19’E longitude) is located
at a distance of about 115 km from the northern coast of Bay of Bengal.
The farm is situated in the red and laterite agroclimatic zone of West Bengal,
India, at an altitude of 48 m above the mean sea level. It is located on the
gently sloping drainage basin of the river Kansai.

The climate of Kharagpur is subhumid, subtropical. It is characterized
by a hot and humid climate in the months of April and May; a rainy climate
from June to September; a moderately hot and dry climate during October
and November; a cool and dry climate in December and January; and a mod-
erate climate in February and March. The site receives an average rainfall of
1200 mm with an occurrence of 70-75% of the total rainfall in the monsoon
months from June to October. The average temperature ranges from 21°C
to 32°C, with a maximum of 47°C in the months of April and May. The
average rainfall is highest during monsoons (June-September), and winter
(November-—January) is generally dry (Figure 1).

The soil of the experimental site was taxonomically grouped under
the great group Haplustalf, which is acid lateritic sandy loam in nature.
Geographically, the soil is an old alluvium laterite derived from the
Chotanagpur Hills in the Quaternary period. It has low organic matter con-
tent and cation exchange capacity (CEC), but high bulk density and saturated
hydraulic conductivity, low nutrient and water retention capacity, and high
permeability (Table 1). The basic infiltration rate of the soil is 6.0 mm/hr.
