Application of intensive tillage (t,) significantly increased the grain yield
of wheat following both TR (Table 7) and DR (Table 8) over that for less
intensive tillage (ty) in both years. After harvest of rice, the puddled and
intensely tilled soil usually turns compacted due to drying and sometimes
forms a surface-soil crust; intensive dry tillage for wheat helps break down
the clods/crust and lets soil become friable for root growth of succeeding
wheat crop (So & Woodhead 1987). This is even clear from the data of
bulk density that intensive tillage (t,) was more effective in reducing the BD
after intensive rice tillage (Table 5). An intensive tillage for wheat following
intensive rice tillage, which broke down the soil pan developed by rice
tillage, made soil suitable for wheat growth (Gajri, Arora, & Prihar 1992).
A suitable tillage can be recommended as a better soil-management option
than a zero or minimum tillage for strength and crusting-susceptible soil (De
Costa & Sangakkara 2006).
