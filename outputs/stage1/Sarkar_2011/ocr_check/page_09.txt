The seasonal variation in SOC was relatively higher under TR-W-F than
DR-W-F (Figure 2 and Figure 3). At the end of the experimental period
(October 2003), the differences in SOC among different residue treatments
were less under TR-W-F;, however, under DR-W-F the SOC values remained
distinctly higher with various residue treatments and highest with ryw,.
Under DR-W-F, the decreases in SOC during monsoon and winter seasons
were comparatively less. Greater decreases of SOC under TR-W-F than DR-
W-F might be ascribed to faster decomposition of organic matter by the
heterotrophic bacteria under wet soil moisture regime that prevails under
TR-W-F, where intensive wet tillage was practiced to conserve moisture.
Increase in soil-moisture content up to a certain extent generally increases
the rate of decomposition of soil organic matter, organic matter content in
soil, and carbon sequestration (Wang, Amundson, & Niu 2000; Verstraeten
et al. 2006; Krull, Skjemstad, & Baldock 2008). Comparatively, higher vari-
ation in SOC under DR-W-F than TR-W-F at the end of the experimental
period (October 2003) was mainly due to the difference in decomposi-
tion rates at variable seasonal soil moisture contents under less intensive
dry tillage treatments than intensive wet tillage treatments. The availability
of soil moisture was eventually higher under TR-W-F system than DR-W-
F systems due to application of more intensive wet tillage treatments as
described in the later part of this article. These phenomena clearly demon-
strate the fact that agricultural management practices such as tillage and
particular climate/soil conditions have a definite impact on decomposition
of soil organic matter (Doran & Smith 1987; Stockdale & Brookes 2006).
