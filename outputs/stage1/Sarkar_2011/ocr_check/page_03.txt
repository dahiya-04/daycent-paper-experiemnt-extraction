The continuous practice of rice-wheat system has resulted in over-
exploitation of the soil resource, which must be reversed by selection
of favorable management practices. Soil quality is strongly related to soil
organic carbon content, which influences the biological, physical, and chem-
ical properties of soil (Ouedraogo, Mando, & Stoosnijder 2006). Soil organic
carbon is also the most frequently reported indicator of soil quality and
agronomic sustainability (Reeves 1997). The crop residue produced from the
rice-wheat system is mostly burned or used as fodder in India. The burning
of residues emits trace gases and particulate matters in the air, resulting in
loss of plant nutrients such as N and C and also diminishes soil biota from
soil surface. The emission of greenhouse gases, such as CHy, CO, N.O and
NOx (x = 1 or 2), has been estimated as 110; 2,306; 2; and 84 Gg, respec-
tively, from rice and wheat straw burning in India, which were 7.8%, 7.8%,
4.5% and 7.7% higher than the emissions in 1994, respectively (Gupta et al.
2004). Instead of burning, application of the rice and wheat residues to the
cropland can be a beneficial practice to sustain soil quality, as soil organic
carbon enrichment has a direct connection with the crop residue application
to the soil (Rasmussen & Parton 1994). The rice-wheat system in India annu-
ally produces 10-14 Mg ha™! crop residues (Samra, Singh, & Kumar 2003),
and returning these residues back to the soil would be an excellent way to
make the system both ecologically and economically sound.

The impact of tillage on soil organic matter (SOM) content was found to
vary greatly with different factors such as crop rotations, residue management,
and climate (Reicosky et al. 1995). Moreover, most of the studies are reported
from temperate and tropical climate zones, revealing a lack of results from
subtropical climates, especially under the rice-wheat system. The effects of
tillage and crop residue on soil fertility and crop yield are also not clearly
understood; the effects vary with soil type, antecedent soil properties, type
and depth of tillage, and various agro-ecological conditions (Balaguru 2008).
