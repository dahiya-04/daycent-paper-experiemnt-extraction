Materials and methods
[p2] 2.1. Experimental site and tillage practices
[p2] The field experiment was conducted at the agricultural research farm of the Institute of Agricultural Sciences, Banaras Hindu Univer- sity, Varanasi, India where a long term experiment on different crop establishment methods in rice (cv PHB-71)–wheat (cv HD 2824) sequence was conducted during 2003–2010 under all India Coor- dinated Research Project on Integrated Farming Systems. The site is located in eastern Indo-Gangetic plains at 25.14°N latitude, 82.03°E longitude and 76.19 m above mean sea level. The experimental soil (inceptisol) is sandy loam in texture. Soil properties on the date of sowing/transplantation of rice in 2003 (at the start of tillage experiment)
[p2] and in 2009 are presented in Table 1. The experiment employed four permutations of tillage and no tillage practices in rice–wheat system. Under no tillage, harvesting of previous crop was done manually leaving 20 cm straw standing. Minimal soil dis- turbance took place under this practice. Leftover plant residues decompose slowly with time. Seeds of the next crop were sown between the standing residues with a zero till drill. In conventional tillage practice, crops were harvested manually that removed crop just above the soil leaving less than 5 cm shoot along with roots, which get mixed with the soil while ploughing the field for the next crop. The treatments involved combination of two tillage practices in
[p2] rice as well as wheat. In rice, two tillage practices were trans- planting of rice on puddle bed with repeated tillage accompanied with final tilling and planking in standing water (RCT) and direct sowing of rice using zero till drill (RNT). Whereas in wheat, the two tillage practices comprised of conventional tillage with one ploughing by disk plough and two harrowings at approximately 30 cm depth by cultivator followed by planking (WCT) and zero till drill sowing of wheat (WNT). The four treatments in the study were: (i) conventional tilling and puddling before transplanting of rice and conventional tilling before sowing of wheat (RCT-WCT), (ii) conventional tilling and puddling before rice transplanting and no tilling before sowing of wheat (RCT-WNT), (iii) no tilling before rice sowing and conventional tilling before wheat sowing (RNT- WCT), and (iv) no tilling before sowing of both rice and wheat (RNT-WNT). Net size of each plot was 5.25 m × 5.25 m and the plots were prepared in triplicates for each tillage permutation. [p3] 2.2. Raising of crops
[p3] Experiments were conducted during 2009 and 2010 in the same well drained field as used in previous years on the same rice culti- var (PHB-71). During both the years of experiment, sowing of rice in RNT-WCT and RNT-WNT was done on 20th June. On the same day, seeds were soaked for the nursery raising of rice to be transplanted in RCT-WNT and RCT-WCT. Seedlings were transplanted in puddled bed on 16th and 19th July in 2009 and 2010, respectively. Recom- mended doses of nutrients (150 kg N + 75 kg P2O5 + 75 kg K2O) were applied through 15:15:15 NPK complex fertilizer and urea. Full doses
[p3] of P and K and 50% of N was applied through 15:15:15 NPK complex at the time of sowing or transplanting. Rest of 75 kg N was applied as urea in two equal splits at critical tillering and panical ini- tiation stages. Herbicides were used as and when required. In no till drill sown rice (RNT-WCT and RNT-WNT), pre emergence spray of pendimethalin 30 EC was applied at the rate of 1 kg ha−1. However, in hand transplanted rice (RCT-WCT and RCT-WNT), pre emergence application of Butachlor 50 EC was done at 1.25 kg ha−1. Herbicide application
[p3] was accompanied with one hand weeding at 25 days after sowing/transplanting. This practice was followed every year since start of tillage experiment. After attaining the maturity, the crops were harvested on 30th October in 2009 and 3rd November in 2010.

In the present paper, days are counted from the transplantation in all the treatments for simplicity. [p4] Soil sampling and analysis
[p4] Oxido-reduction potential (ORP) of soil was measured in 2010 using Double junction ORP Tester 10 (Eutech Instruments, Mum- bai, India). The instrument was first calibrated in the laboratory with reference Zobbel buffer to 245 mV. Soil temperature was mea- sured
[p4] with the help of a mercury thermometer (1/10 °C). Both measurements were taken at 10 cm depth. For bulk density soil was collected up to 15 cm depth using the soil corer (6.5 cm diameter). For other analyses, soils from 15 cm depth were collected from four different places randomly in each treatment with the help of auger. The collected soils were mixed together, a subsample was used for the analyses of moisture content and rest was air dried and ground to pass through 2 mm sieve. Soil organic carbon and total nitrogen were determined following Walkley and Black (1947) and micro Kjeldahl digestion method of Bremner and Mulvaney (1982) using Gerhardt Automatic N Analyzer, Frankfurt, Germany, respectively. Available
[p4] phosphorous was extracted following Olsen et al. (1954) and estimated by the method of Dickman and Bray (1940).
[p4] 2.6. Statistical analysis
[p4] All the observations were recorded in triplicates. Their means and standard errors were calculated. Three-way ANOVA was car- ried out to check the interactions of tillage treatments, year of experiment and stage of rice plant. One-way ANOVA with Duncan’s Multiple Range Test and Student’s t-test at 5% level of significance were used to check differences between means. Linear regres- sion analysis was done to identify the relationships between the
