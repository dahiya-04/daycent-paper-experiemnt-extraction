METHODS AND MATERIALS
[p4] Experimental Site
[p4] A field investigation was carried out at the experimental farm of Agricultural & Food Engineering Department, Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur, India. Kharagpur (22°19′N latitude; 87°19′E longitude) is located at a distance of about 115 km from the northern coast of Bay of Bengal. The farm is situated in the red and laterite agroclimatic zone of West Bengal, India, at an altitude of 48 m above the mean sea level. It is located on the gently sloping drainage basin of the river Kansai.
[p4] The climate of Kharagpur is subhumid, subtropical. It is characterized by a hot and humid climate in the months of April and May; a rainy climate from June to September; a moderately hot and dry climate during October and November; a cool and dry climate in December and January; and a mod- erate climate in February and March. The site receives an average rainfall of 1200 mm with an occurrence of 70–75% of the total rainfall in the monsoon months from June to October. The average temperature ranges from 21°C to 32°C, with a maximum of 47°C in the months of April and May. The average rainfall is highest during monsoons (June–September), and winter (November–January) is generally dry (Figure 1).
[p4] The soil of the experimental site was taxonomically grouped under the great group Haplustalf, which is acid lateritic sandy loam in nature. Geographically, the soil is an old alluvium laterite derived from the Chotanagpur Hills in the Quaternary period. It has low organic matter con- tent and cation exchange capacity (CEC), but high bulk density and saturated hydraulic conductivity, low nutrient and water retention capacity, and high permeability (Table 1). The basic infiltration rate of the soil is 6.0 mm/hr.

[p6] Soil Analyses
[p6] The experimental soil was analyzed for basic chemical and physical prop- erties following standard methods as follows: pH in water was measured by glass electrode and cation exchange capacity following the neutral normal ammonium acetate extraction procedure (Jackson 1967; Hesse 1994; Table 1).
[p6] Soil Organic Carbon
[p6] The impacts of puddling, tillage, and crop-residue incorporation, along with rate of inorganic N application for rice-wheat system in changing soil pro- ductivity, were evaluated by assessing temporal organic carbon content of soil. Soil samples were collected from 0–15, 150–30, and 30–45 cm depth from each treatment combination at the end of three years of the experi- ment (October 2003), processed, and kept in a dry place for further analysis. Organic carbon content of soil was estimated by the modified Walkley-Black method (Walkley & Black 1934).

Bulk density was measured following the principles of Blake (Blake 1965) and saturated hydraulic conductivity by Laboratorium permeameter following the principle of Klute (Klute 1965).
[p7] Soil strength (SS) or soil compaction was measured after harvest of each rice and wheat crop to know the effect of tillage and crop-residue applica- tion on crop-root development and crop growth (Hasegawa et al. 1985; Busscher et al. 2000). The SS was measured at three randomly selected loca- tions in each treatment plot and averaged to get more accurate values. Three readings of SS at 2.54 cm, 7.62 cm, and 12.7 cm depth were measured from each location. Soil strength measurement was performed with a manually operated cone penetrometer equipped with a 30°cone having a 95-sq.-cm basal area (Singh & Malhi 2006). All of the measurements of soil strength were taken just after harvest of rice and wheat crops when moisture content of all the soil layers was near field capacity.
[p7] Double ring infiltrometer was used to measure the infiltration rate of the soil (Bouwer 1986). The diameters of outer and inner rings of the infil- trometer were 30 and 55 cm, respectively. The infiltrometers were placed in plots of all tillage-crop residue combinations after harvest of rice crop (2003) and measurement was taken at 30, 60, 120, 150, 195, 210, 240, and 300 min, until the steady-state infiltration rate was achieved.
[p7] Statistical Analyses
[p7] The effects of tillage, crop residue, and N application rate on the final status of SOC were assessed by measuring soil organic carbon at the end of the experimental period (October 2003) under all the treatment combinations in both TR-W-F and DR-W-F. The data obtained were statistically analyzed by M-STATC software package and presented in the form of analyses of variance (ANOVA) (Russel & Einsensmith 1986). ANOVA was also performed to assess the effect of tillage and crop-residue application on yield of rice and wheat crops under average rate of inorganic N application. The differences between treatment means were tested using critical difference value at 5% (CD0.05) and 1% (CD0.01) levels of significance. Linear regression relationships among soil organic carbon level and rate of N application were established using Microsoft Excel (2003).
