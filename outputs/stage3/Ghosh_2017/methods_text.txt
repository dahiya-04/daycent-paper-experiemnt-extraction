Material and Methods
[p2] Soil and Site Characteristics
[p2] The data reported here were generated from two long-term experiments carried out at main research farm of the ICAR–Indian Institute of Pulses Research (ICAR–IIPR), Kanpur, India (26270N, 80140E and 152.4 m above mean sea level). The soils of study site belong to taxonomic class Typic Ustochrept (Inceptisol) with sandy-loam texture. The climate of the research site comes in sub-humid tropical, receives an annual rainfall of 722 mm with the mean annual maximum and minimum temperature is 33.0 and 20.0 C, respectively. At the initiation of the experiment, the SOC was 2.8 g kg-1 soil in upland and lowland experiment.
[p2] Experimental Design and Treatments
[p2] These two long-term experiments were initiated during the rainy season (July) of 2003 with a major objective to investigate the effect of different cropping systems and nutrient management practices on soil health and crop productivity. Both the experiments were laid in a split-plot design with three replications; and the treatment comprised of four cropping systems in the main-plot and three nutrient managements in subplot in each year. In the present study, the authors emphasized to unfold the impact of chickpea inclusion in cereal–cereal systems. Therefore, the authors selected alternate-year chickpea included rotation and conventional cereal–cereal rotation from both production system. Accordingly, the authors separated (1) maize– wheat (MW), (2) maize–wheat–maize–chickpea (MWMC) from upland; and (1) rice–wheat (RW), (2) rice–wheat– rice–chickpea (RWRC) from lowland system. Both the long-term experiments had common nutrient management practices viz., (1) control (2) inorganic (NPKSZnB) and (3) organic (all crop residues incorporation ? biofertiliz- ers ? farmyard manure (FYM) with 5 Mg ha-1). The cultivar Azad Uttam (maize), PBW 343 (wheat), and DCP- 92-3 (chickpea) were used in the upland production sys- tem; where, Pant Dhan 12 (rice), PBW 343 (wheat), and KWR-108 (chickpea) were used in the lowland rice-based experiment. Fertilizer nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P2O5) and potassium (K2O) (kg ha-1) were applied in the form of urea, diammonium phosphate and muriate of potash respectively; and the detailed rate is presented in Table 1. [p3] Soil Sampling and Analysis
[p3] Soil samples were collected from four soil depths (0–0.2, 0.2–0.4, 0.4–0.6, 0.6–0.8 m). Soil samples were taken with post-hole auger from four random spots in each plot (three replications) after harvesting the last crop in the crop cycle. The sampled soils were air dried, ground followed by passing through a 2 mm sieve and stored at room tem- perature for analyzing oxidisable organic C by wet oxida- tion method [12]. Total organic C was calculated using the formula: oxidizable organic carbon 9 1.33 [13]. In surface and sub-surface depths, organic C-fractions were analyzed through modified Walkley and Black method as described by Chan et al. [14]. The concentrated H2SO4 of 5, 10 and 20 ml resulted in three acid aqueous solution ratios of 0.5:1, 1:1, and 2:1 corresponding to 12 N, 18 N and 24 N
[p3] of H2SO4, respectively [15]. The organic C determined using 5, 10 and 20 ml of concentrated H2SO4, when compared with TOC allowed separation of TOC into fol- lowing four different fractions of decreasing oxidisability: fraction I (Cfrac1), organic C oxidisable under 12 N H2SO4; fraction II (Cfrac2), the difference in organic C extracted between 18 N and 12 N H2SO4; fraction III (Cfrac3), the difference in organic C extracted between 18 N and 24 N H2SO4 (24 N H2SO4 is equivalent to the standard Walkley and Black method); and fraction IV (Cfrac4), residual organic C after reaction with 24 N H2SO4 when compared with TOC [16].

[p4] Particulate Organic Carbon (POC)
[p4] Particulate organic C was analyzed taking after the strategies depicted by Sollins et al. [18]. Briefly, 20 g soil was defloc- culated by adding 100 ml of sodium hexametaphosphate. The soil solution was shaken for 18 h with 90 rpm min-1 in a reciprocating shaker. The suspension was washed with 53 lm screen with the consistent stream of distilled water. The rest of the soil on the screen was washed, collected, and dried (60 C for 12 h) for estimation of POC.
